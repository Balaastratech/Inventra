# The Reorder Journey

## Inventra, in plain English

Whenever Inventra thinks a product might run out, it walks through the same six checks, automatically and in order, before any money is spent.

> **Safety rule:** The system can say **no** on its own. It can never say **yes** on its own. A named person has to approve every purchase.

## The six checks

### 1. Is this product actually running low?
The system looks at how much stock is on hand and how fast it is selling. If coverage is healthy, the journey stops here.

### 2. Who can supply it, and how fast?
Real suppliers are compared on price, delivery time, and track record, not on supplier sales claims.

### 3. Does this fit our budget and rules?
The proposed order is checked against the remaining monthly budget and the company's buying policy.

### 4. A named person signs off
Everything gathered so far - the numbers, supplier choice, and cost - is shown to a human. Nothing moves forward until someone approves it by name.

**This is the only step that waits on a person.**

### 5. One last check before buying
Because approval may sit for a while, Inventra re-checks that price, stock, and budget have not changed before purchase.

### 6. The order is placed - safely, once
Only now is the purchase created. Duplicate-safety prevents the same approved request from being accidentally placed more than once.

## How a journey can end

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| No action needed | Stock was healthy at step 1. |
| More information needed | Something was missing or unclear, so the system asks rather than guesses. |
| Stopped | A check failed: stale data, no reliable supplier, over budget, or a human said no. |
| Order placed | Every check passed and a named person approved it. |

## Auditability

Every step leaves a paper trail: who checked what, and when. If a manager asks why something happened, the system is intended to have the answer on record.
